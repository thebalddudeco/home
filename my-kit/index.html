01 / NANUK

NANUK 935
Pro Photo Kit

The case that comes with me.

Anytime I’m going anywhere on a shoot, the 935 is coming with me. It gives me enough room for a serious amount of equipment without worrying about everything getting crushed, wet, or thrown around during transportation. The orange color makes it easy to spot when a room is full of black cases and bags.

The Pro Photo Kit setup keeps the equipment organized instead of turning one large case into a gear hunt. I can get to what I need without digging through the entire thing. It feels solid, and after using it regularly, I trust it with my equipment. It has become part of my standard setup—not something I only bring out for bigger jobs.

The only thing I wish NANUK offered is an air-filled wheel option. New York sidewalks are rough: uneven pavement, cracked concrete, curbs, subway entrances. A little more cushion would make a heavy case even easier to move. Besides that, the 935 has been one of the most dependable cases I own.

02 / NANUK

NANUK 910

A dedicated home for the Tilta Hydra Alien Mini Pro.

The 910 is the case I use for my Tilta Hydra Alien Mini Pro. The Hydra has multiple parts and is not something I want loose inside a regular camera bag. Keeping it in its own case means the setup stays together and protected between locations.

The cubed foam is the reason this works so well. I customized the inside around the Hydra, so the equipment stays where I put it and I can confirm that every part is there before leaving a job. It is compact, easy to handle, easy to store, and gives one very specific setup a dependable home.

NANUK 905

Small protection for a smaller kit.

The 905 is the smaller case in my setup. I use it when I don’t need one of the larger cases and want protected space for smaller equipment and accessories without carrying the 935 just for a few items.

The cubed foam lets me set the interior up around whatever I’m carrying instead of letting smaller gear move around loose. I also like that the orange color is easy to identify. It fills a specific role: compact, solid protection that is easy to store and simple to grab.

NANUK 940

The hardware case.

The 940 is where I keep my C-stand knuckles, super clamps, and Joby arms. That equipment is heavy, awkward, and easy to scatter across different bags. A dedicated case means I know exactly where the production hardware is when I need it.

The Pro Photo Kit setup gives those accessories a real system instead of a pile of metal in an empty box. It is not a case I take every time I leave the house, but for production work it keeps the heavier accessories separate from the camera gear and makes the whole setup easier to manage.

N-PVD 18L

The everyday backpack, right beside the 935.

The N-PVD 18L is the backpack I use the most. Anytime I’m going somewhere on a shoot, I usually have it with me. It carries what I’m actively using without feeling like I’m hauling a giant camera bag, which makes it useful for production days and regular everyday work.

I keep the essentials in the backpack and leave the larger equipment in the 935. The N-CUBIK T1 pouch keeps smaller accessories from disappearing inside the bag. The N-PVD 18L is not something I bought and forgot about—it is one of the main parts of my everyday photography setup.

07 / FUJIFILM

Fujifilm
X-H2

The body I build the working kit around.

The Fujifilm X-H2 is one of the cameras I rely on the most. It gives me the resolution I want for photography, but it also has the features I need when I’m shooting video or working on a production where one camera has to handle different jobs.

What I like most is how flexible it is. I can use it for portraits, events, street photography, product work, and video without feeling like I’m using the wrong tool. The files give me room to crop and edit, and the body feels solid enough for real working situations.

It is not the smallest camera, and it is not trying to be. That is part of the appeal. It feels dependable in my hands, especially with a cage, external accessories, or larger lenses.

AF 85mm
f/1.8 STM

Compression and separation for portraits.

The Viltrox AF 85mm f/1.8 is the lens I reach for when I want a more compressed look and separation between my subject and the background. On the X-H2 it works especially well for portraits and tighter details.

The 85mm focal length requires room to step back, so it is not the lens I use for everything. But when I have that space, it gives me the perspective and separation I’m looking for.
